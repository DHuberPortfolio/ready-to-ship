How it works

Why it’s built this way

A yes has a bar

Under every question is its bar: one to three things that have to be true, each with its own tick. All ticked is a yes; anything less is a no. A no isn’t a failing grade: it’s the next thing to do, and the scorecard says how to start.

No “partly”

Each bar is either cleared or it isn’t, so “partly” is a no. Survey research finds that an easy way out, such as “not sure,” gets picked to skip thinking the question through. Here, not sure means no evidence, and no evidence means no.

One gap is enough

There’s no “10 of 12, so mostly ready.” Each question guards against a different way of failing silently, so a strong answer on one can’t cover for another. Google’s ML Test Score takes the lowest section score for the same reason.

Ready for what?

A workflow where a person approves everything needs four of these, one of which tests the reviewers themselves. One that lets outputs go out alone needs up to twelve. When the design you planned isn’t ready, the scorecard lists what the cautious one would need, so the work can start while you close the gaps.

Numbers over opinions

Question 5 can only be a yes when your limit and counts are filled in and the arithmetic agrees, and question 11 gets a spot-check plan worked out from your limit. Both use the same likely range as my error-rate calculator and eval harness, so all three agree to the decimal.

Where the questions come from

From failures I found when I measured two AI workflows I’d built, checked against NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework, the EU AI Act, OWASP’s list of LLM risks, and research on checklists, automation and statistics. The method gives the reasoning and every source.

What it doesn’t check

Whether you’re allowed to use the data, security beyond basic prompt injection, legal compliance, or fairness between groups of people. It checks one thing: whether wrong outputs get stopped or caught, and, where some get through, whether you’d know how many.

Private by design

Everything runs in your browser. The link to your answers holds yes and no and your numbers, never the workflow’s name or your notes. Run the tests behind it with npm test.
